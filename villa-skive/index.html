Residence concept · Rev A · Sunnmøre coast, Norway

Villa Skive

A 64-metre drum of pale Norwegian granite on a headland above the sea. The outside is French: rusticated base, pilasters, cornices, a slate mansard with round œil-de-bœuf windows. The seaward side breaks the rules. Two white discs slide out of the stone and hang over the cliff, the way Stark's Malibu house does. Skive is Norwegian for disc.

The idea

Three buildings in one

The disc

A ring around a winter garden

The plan is a perfect circle 64 m across with a 26 m courtyard at the centre, covered by a shallow glass dome on a black steel grid. Every room is on the ring, so every room gets daylight from two sides: the landscape outside and the garden inside. A 4 m gallery runs the full circle on the courtyard side, so you can walk the whole house in one loop of about 100 m.

The French soul

A château facade, bent into a circle

The outer wall follows the rules of an 18th-century hôtel particulier. It has a rusticated stone base, a tall piano nobile with 6 m French windows, a smaller upper floor, a heavy cornice and a slate mansard roof. There are 48 equal bays at 7.5° each. Every window is black steel with square 600 mm panes. The window is the one detail that repeats everywhere, and it ties the old and the new together.

The Stark edge

Two discs over the cliff

On the sea side the stone stops. A 70° sweep of floor-to-ceiling glass opens the Grand Salon, and two post-tensioned concrete discs slide out through the facade. The lower one carries a 22 m infinity pool and cantilevers 14 m past the cliff edge. The upper one is the master suite's terrace. Under the house, the Lab is a car and workshop level cut into the rock, with an inclined lift down to a boathouse at sea level.

The window

This is the window you described: very large, very classical, very black. Each one is a pair of 6 m French doors made of slim, thermally broken steel. The doors are split into 600 mm squares by true glazing bars, not stick-on ones. The steel profile is 40 mm wide on the face, so from a few metres away the grid reads like a fine black drawing on the glass.

The stone around the window is pure 1780s Paris: a granite architrave, a keystone, a projecting cornice above and an iron balconette across the bottom. Behind the old-looking face the glass is modern. It is triple glazed with warm-edge spacers and Ug ≈ 0.5 W/m²K, which a house at 62° N needs.

Materials

Norwegian stone, French manners

Iddefjord granite

Pale grey, honed. Ashlar walls, pilasters, cornices and window surrounds. Quarried in Østfold.

Rusticated granite

Same stone, split-face with deep horizontal joints, for the base storey.

Larvikite

Norway's blue-black stone with a silvery shimmer. Polished floor of the entrance hall and the pool coping.

Otta slate

Dark grey slate hung in fish-scale courses on the mansard, the way Paris roofs do it.

Blackened steel

All windows, the dome grid, the balconettes and the glass balustrade shoes.

White concrete

Post-tensioned, board-marked. Used only for the two cantilevered discs, so they read as new objects pushed through the old stone.

Reality check

What has to be solved before this is real

Planning risk

The 100-metre shoreline rule

Norway's Planning and Building Act §1-8 generally bans building within 100 m of the sea unless the municipal plan sets another building line. A seafront site needs a zoning plan or a dispensation. Pick the site with this in mind, or move the house to a highland lake or valley edge where the rule works differently.

Engineering

14 m cantilevers in coastal wind

The discs need post-tensioned concrete with back-spans anchored into the Lab walls. The pool adds about 150 t of water at the tip. Exposed west-coast wind and salt spray call for dynamic analysis and stainless reinforcement at the edges.

Energy

Glass area versus TEK17

The simple compliance route in TEK17 caps windows and doors at about 25 % of heated floor area. This design will exceed that, so it needs a full energy calculation. Use ground-source heat pumps through the bedrock and very good walls behind the stone.

Snow

The glass dome

A 26 m span on a shallow 6 m rise has to carry snow and drifts. Use laminated low-iron glass, heated perimeter gutters, and a steel grid sized for drift build-up against the mansard.

Feasible

Stone facade construction

Build it as a concrete or steel frame with 80–120 mm granite cladding on stainless anchors, not as solid load-bearing stone. The curved cornices and pilasters can be CNC-cut from 3D files like this one.

Rough budget

Order of magnitude only

At an assumed NOK 120 000–200 000 per m² for this level of stone, steel and engineering, about 10 900 m² comes to roughly NOK 1.3–2.2 billion before land. This is a guess for scale, not a quote. A Norwegian cost consultant should price it.
