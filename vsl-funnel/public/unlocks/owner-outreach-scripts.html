The owner outreach scripts

Word for word: the letter, the email, the first call and the follow-ups for owners with no successor. Send the agreed number every week and keep a simple log of who you contacted and when.

Who you write to

  • Owners inside your buy box: your industry, your size range, your geography.
  • Owners over 55 with no family member or manager named as the next owner.
  • Businesses that have run for ten years or more and still carry the founder's name.

Fill the [brackets] with your own details. Never claim a deal size, a track record or a buyer group you don't have.

The letter (posted, signed by hand)

Dear [first name], I run [your business], a [industry] business in [area], and I have followed [their business] for some time. I am writing because I would like to buy a business like yours and keep what you built running: the name, the team and the customers. If you have thought about what happens to [their business] when you step back, I would value a private conversation. Nothing is shared with anyone, and there is no broker involved. My direct number is [phone]. I will call your office in the week of [date] in case that is easier. [Your name], [Your business]

The email (a week after the letter)

Subject: [their business] and what comes next Hi [first name], I wrote to you last week about [their business]. I own [your business] in [area], and I am looking to buy one well-run [industry] business and keep it running as it is. If a private conversation about your plans for the next few years is useful, reply with a time that suits you. [Your name], [phone]

The first call

"Hi [first name], it's [your name] from [your business]. I sent you a letter about [their business]. Have you got two minutes? I'll be direct. I'm looking to buy one [industry] business in [area] and keep it running with its team. I'm calling owners who've built something worth keeping. What would you want to happen to the business when you step back?"

Then listen. Write down what they care about: their staff, their customers, their name on the door, their income after the sale. That list shapes the offer.

If they say "not for sale"

"Understood. Most owners I speak to aren't selling today. Can I check back in six months? If anything changes before then, my number is [phone]."

The follow-ups

Day 7 after the call: "Thank you for the time last week. What you said about [their priority] stayed with me. When you want to talk again, I'm here." Day 21: a short note with one useful thing for their industry (a supplier change, a rule change, a local market fact you can stand behind). Every 90 days: one line, "Still keen to talk whenever you are."

When they want to talk

  • Ask for a mutual NDA, then three years of financial statements and tax returns.
  • Run the numbers through the 1.5x test before you discuss price.
  • Agree the shape of the deal with the seller first (commercial debt, seller finance and seller rollover), then take it to the lender.

Education only, not legal, tax or financial advice. Follow the marketing and privacy rules where you send letters and emails.
