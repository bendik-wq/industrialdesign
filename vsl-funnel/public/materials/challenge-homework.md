## Day 1 · Your capacity number

[NOTE: Assigned at the end of Day 1. Takes 20 to 30 minutes. VIP worksheet review covers it.]

This sheet turns the 1.5x test into a deal size. Tomorrow's buy box starts with the number you write here, so do it tonight.

The maths runs on the profit of the business you'd buy, never on your own. Your own profit is your track record and your safety net.

1. Profit last year in your own business, before interest and tax.
2. The profit range you'd want in a first target. If you have no target yet, pick the size that feels right for a first deal.
3. Divide the top of that range by 1.5. The answer is the most the target can pay in debt each year and still pass the test.
4. Multiply line 3 by five. On the illustrative terms from today (bank at 10% over 7 years, seller note at 6% over 4 years, a 70/20/10 split), that's roughly the price the deal can carry.
5. Who runs your business on the days you're in the new one? Write a name, or write "nobody" if that's the truth.
6. How clean are your own books? Do they match your tax returns for the last three years?
7. Write your capacity sentence in your own words.

Illustrative example: "My first deal is a business making $300,000 to $600,000 a year in profit, at a price up to about 3.3 times that profit on a 70/20/10 structure, with a manager or senior staff member who can run it day to day."

Why it matters: a buy box without a size sends you after every business you see. With this number, you'll know which deals are worth a conversation before you have one.

## Day 2 · Your buy box

[NOTE: Assigned at the end of Day 2. Takes 30 to 40 minutes. VIP worksheet review covers it.]

Eight lines on one page that decide what you look at, who you call and what you say no to. Write it as if no particular business existed, then hold any business you already have in mind up against it.

1. Industry, as narrowly as you can say it.
2. Kind of target: competitor, supplier, customer or adjacent. For a first deal, we suggest a competitor.
3. Where: a geography you can actually manage.
4. Size, from your Day 1 capacity: the profit range and the illustrative price that profit can carry.
5. Owner situation: years in the business, no successor, open to a handover.
6. Who runs it after the owner steps back: a manager in place, a senior person who can step up, or nobody yet.
7. Financial standards: three years of statements that match the tax returns, steady or rising profit, and the largest share of revenue you'll accept from one customer.
8. Deal-breakers: the red flags that end a conversation in your industry.

Then write your buy box as two sentences, the way you'd say it to an owner on the phone.

Two checks before you submit. Could someone else build a list of owners from this page without asking you anything? And would you be happy to own every business that passes all eight lines and the test?

Why it matters: tomorrow you list ten owners, and every one of them has to fit this page. Your two sentences also become line three of your first message.

## Day 3 · 10 owners and your first message

[NOTE: Assigned at the end of Day 3. Takes 40 to 60 minutes. VIP worksheet review covers the message.]

A list of ten owners who fit your buy box, and a first message ready for the one at the top. Draft it tonight and send it after Day 4.

Start with the people who know your market: write down every owner you can name from memory, then ask your senior staff, your supplier reps and your accountant. Fill the gaps from directories and company records.

For each of the ten owners, fill in these columns:

| Business | Owner | Where | Estimated size | Years in the business | Signs of no successor | How you're connected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then draft your first message, four lines, for the owner at the top of the list:

1. Who you are, and how you're connected to them.
2. Something true and specific about their business.
3. Why you're writing, about their future and the people they care about.
4. A small, easy ask, such as a coffee.

Leave out price, financing and the word "acquisition".

Next, draft your second touch: two lines, sent two weeks later.

Finally, name the owner you'd contact first, and say why. Add which of the five things owners say you expect to hear, and your answer to it.

Why it matters: owners sell to the buyer who was in touch when they were ready. The list and the message are where that starts, and the weekly rhythm keeps it going.

## Day 4 · Run the test on your first target

[NOTE: Assigned at the end of Day 4. Takes 30 to 45 minutes. VIP worksheet review checks the numbers.]

The full stack and the 1.5x test on the owner at the top of your list, using your best estimates.

1. Rebuilt profit: the seller's profit with verifiable add-backs, less a fair salary for whoever will run it.
2. Price: start at three times the rebuilt profit.
3. The split, with the dollar amount of each piece: commercial debt, seller finance and seller rollover.
4. Yearly payments on each piece and the total. On the illustrative terms, each $1M of bank debt costs about $205,000 a year and each $1M of seller note over four years about $289,000 a year. Rollover carries no yearly payment.
5. Coverage: rebuilt profit divided by total yearly payments.
6. If it's under 1.5x, the levers you'd offer the seller, in order (a longer note, a payment holiday, more rollover, a better price), and the coverage after each. Then your walk-away point.
7. The order of your conversations as four steps, with names: the seller, the test, the lenders, and the advisers who check the letter of intent.

Run the test on the years after any payment holiday, never only on year one. If you add a working capital line, its payments go into line 4 as well.

Why it matters: this is the centre of tomorrow's deal plan. Capacity, buy box and owners tell you which business, and this sheet tells you whether it can pay for itself.

## Day 5 · Your deal plan on one page

[NOTE: Assigned during Day 5, before the offer. Takes about 20 minutes with the other four sheets done.]

One page, seven sections, pulled from your four worksheets. Read it on Monday morning and act on it before lunch.

1. Capacity: the target profit range, the price it can carry at 1.5x, and who runs your business on the days you're in the new one.
2. Buy box: the eight lines and your two sentences.
3. Owners: the ten names, the one you'll contact first, your first message, and the time each week you'll send twenty-five owner contacts.
4. Stack: the price, the split and each piece, plus any working capital line.
5. Test: the yearly payments, the coverage, the levers in order and your walk-away point.
6. Order: seller, test, lenders, letter of intent, with names at each step.
7. First 90 days: weeks 1 and 2 foundations, weeks 3 to 6 outreach and first conversations, weeks 7 to 10 second conversations and the first pass, weeks 11 to 13 shape, lender and LOI.

Then write the next step for your weakest section, with a date.

Three gaps to close before Monday: a capacity built on the seller's best case instead of a rebuilt profit; a list with an empty connections column; and a stack with no rollover.

Why it matters: a plan written down and submitted is a commitment you've made to yourself. Whichever way you run it, start with the first message on Monday.
