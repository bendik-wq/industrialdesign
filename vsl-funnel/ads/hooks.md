# G&L hook bank: "1 in 1"-class openers

What makes "the odds of you dying? 1 in 1" work: (1) a question nobody's asked them out loud,
(2) an answer that is **mathematically certain**, so there is nothing to argue with, (3) mortality or
loss sitting just under it, and (4) a pivot that turns the certainty into an *opportunity*. Each
hook below has the certainty, the pivot, and the belief it shifts. All are true as stated and
use no proof beyond the approved list. None asserts a personal attribute (Meta).

## A. Certainty hooks (the "1 in 1" family)
| # | Hook (cards) | Pivot | Belief it shifts |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Do you know the odds you'll leave your business one day? / 1 in 1. | Every owner on your street will too. Somebody's going to own what they built. Why not you? | "Exit is the end" → every exit is someone's entry |
| A2 | The odds your biggest competitor walks away one day? / 1 in 1. | Their customers, team and phone number go to *someone*. | Competitor = threat → competitor = acquisition |
| A3 | Every business you drive past today / will have a new owner one day. / Every single one. | Most owners never ask who. | Businesses are fixed scenery → they're inventory |
| A4 | Every sign on your street / will one day carry someone else's name. | Some of them could carry yours. | (Visual: the sign over the gate, the doctrine's motif) |
| A5 | 100% of business owners stop working one day. / Not 99. 100. | The only choice is what you own when you do. | Retirement planning → acquisition planning |
| A6 | How many businesses will you build in your life? / Probably one. / How many could you own? | There's no rule that says you can only build. | Linear growth → stacked ownership |

## B. Time hooks (the strongest belief shift: time *can* be bought)
| # | Hook | Pivot | Belief |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Time is the one thing money can't buy. / Everyone says so. / They're wrong. | Someone already spent 25 years building it. You can buy those years. | Growth takes decades → growth can be bought |
| B2 | It took you ten years to get here. / How long will the next ten take? | Unless the next ten already exist, in a business down the road. | Next decade = repeat the grind |
| B3 | Count your Saturdays. / 52 a year. / How many have you given the business? | (52-square grid fills) The owners who buy their growth get some back. | Hours = growth |
| B4 | You can buy a house that someone else built. / Nobody thinks twice. / Why not a business? | The customers, team and name already exist. | Businesses must be built → they can be bought like property |

## C. Inversion hooks (Ancient-Greece-style "everyone believes X, history says Y")
| # | Hook | Pivot | Belief |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The biggest companies in history / weren't only built. / They were bought. | Rockefeller bought out rival refineries. Buffett buys whole companies. | Real founders build from scratch |
| C2 | Banks lend to buy businesses every day. / So why does nobody tell owners? | Commercial debt, seller finance, seller rollover. | Buying = only for the rich |
| C3 | The person selling a business / can be the one who finances it. | It's called seller finance. Josh Li's first two deals were 100% seller financed. | You need cash to buy |
| C4 | Nobody asks a 60-year-old owner / "who gets this when you stop?" / Somebody should. | You could be the one asking. | Deals are found on marketplaces |

## D. Mortality-adjacent (use sparingly, kept gentle; owners under stress are a vulnerable audience)
| # | Hook | Pivot |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | One day someone will clear out your desk. / What will they find there? | A job you owned, or a group you built? |
| D2 | You will hand over the keys one day. / The only question is how many sets you'll have. | (Key motif: one key → a ring of keys) |

## Endings that close the loop (bookends)
- "The odds you'll leave your business are 1 in 1. But the odds you're the one buying? That part starts here."
- "The years are for sale. Most owners never ask the price."
- "Every sign changes one day. Make sure one of them is yours."

## Rules applied
- No income or result promises; the only results are Josh's approved facts, attributed.
- No fake scarcity, no "free". The CTA is "Apply for a strategy call."
- No callouts of age, finances or other personal attributes ("Are you over 50?" is out).
- History claims are kept to well-documented facts: Standard Oil bought out rival refineries; Berkshire Hathaway acquires whole companies.
