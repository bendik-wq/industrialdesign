# G&L cold-traffic ad brief

Built with the four-sales method: an ad for cold traffic sells four things before it sells the offer (the content, hope, value, impulse), using 17 "reasons why". Process: premises → target → brief → writing. The brief decides *what* is said; a reference only decides order and rhythm.

What we keep from the method: all four sales and all 17 elements. What we drop: fake scarcity (no "stock running out", no countdowns) and anything sexualised. Everything else stays.

---

## Step 1 · Premises (what we already know)

1. **Brand:** G&L M&A Advisory. Co-founder Josh Li.
2. **Product:** an M&A advisory engagement that helps owners grow by buying companies: deal sourcing, structuring, financing, closing, and running what they buy.
3. **Mechanism components:** bank debt lent against the target's cash flow; seller finance (vendor note); seller rollover (the seller keeps a slice); the 1.5x debt service cover test; off-market, direct-to-owner sourcing; a board for credibility; an operating system for the sites you buy. Overfinancing (finance above the price) where a deal supports it.
4. **Who it's for:** owners of profitable businesses doing $1M to $100M a year, US and Australia; best fit $3M to $50M+.
5. **Price:** the engagement fee exists and is covered on the strategy call (not in the ad).
6. **The ask:** apply; if it's a fit, the team books a strategy call. Never "free".
7. **Proof (approved):** Josh, unemployed with no experience, about 1,000 cold calls and four dead deals; first deal at 25, 100% seller financed over 7 years at 4%, none of his own money, about 13,000 customers on day one, about $150K out in year one; youngest in Australian history to do a fully seller-financed healthcare deal; first two acquisitions both 100% seller financed; JC Health Group a multi seven-figure portfolio. Member result: a $2.4M deal financed above the price ("their result, not a promise of yours").
8. **Proof (from Bendik, needs sign-off) †:** Josh's first two deals had no personal guarantee and no security. A healthcare group owner with dozens of sites whose last ten acquisitions were unprofitable.
9. **What they gain by acting now (true):** the deals that exist today (owners stopping, businesses that will never list) go to whoever asks first.
10. **What they lose by waiting (true):** another year buying customers one at a time; the business they wanted bought by a competitor or private equity.

## Step 2 · Target

**Market:** business owners who want to grow revenue and profit. They know growth is the problem, they've tried the known solutions (ads, agencies, hires, new locations), and they're frustrated and sceptical.

**Segments (one per ad):**
| # | Segment | What they already suspect |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Owners stuck on a plateau, burning money on ads and agencies | "Growth keeps getting more expensive." |
| S2 | Owners who believe buying a business needs millions in cash | "I'd need a war chest to buy anything." |
| S3 | Owners who fear a second business would break them | "I can barely run the one I have." |
| S4 | Owners already doing acquisitions that disappoint | "The deals look good, then don't make money." |
| S5 | Owners expanding into new cities, states or countries | "Opening a new market takes years." |

**Avatar (shared layer):** owner-operator, 35 to 60, runs a profitable $1M to $100M business they built themselves; US or Australia; married, the business carries the family; proud of what they built and quietly exhausted by it. Watches business podcasts, founder stories and deal breakdowns; respects people who've done it; distrusts gurus and "make money online". Enemies: agencies that never deliver, banks that say no, brokers. Allies: their accountant, other owners. Language: "cash flow", "margins", "my guys", "the business", "scale", "exit".

- **Primary desire:** grow faster without working more.
- **Problem:** every path to growth is slow, expensive and runs through them.
- **Secondary desires:** time back, a business that runs without them, a bigger exit, respect as a builder.
- **Pain points:** rising ad costs, hires who don't work out, Saturdays at the desk, revenue that's flat while costs rise.
- **Dream:** a group of businesses run by a team, money coming in from several places, a sale at a group multiple.
- **Fears:** losing what they built on a bad deal, personal guarantees on their house, being the bottleneck forever, a competitor getting big first.

## Step 3 · Brief (the 17 elements)

### Sale 1 · Content
1. **Format:** cinematic narration (deep, aristocratic British voice) over film imagery. Reads as a short documentary, not an ad. Guard down.
2. **Topic:** the hidden way the biggest companies grow, and why owners are never told it.
3. **Concept:** "There's a second door to growth almost nobody uses." Each ad is one scene that proves it.
4. **Visual hook:** a single striking image tied to the controlling metaphor (two doors; a call log; a map with one pin; ten red lines on a spreadsheet).
5. **Copy hook:** a certainty or a reversal the viewer can't argue with in the first five seconds.

### Sale 2 · Hope
6. **Identification:** "You built something real, and every path to grow it now runs through you."
7. **Point of contact:** growth keeps getting more expensive: ads cost more, hires take longer, every customer costs more than the last.
8. **Unique mechanism of the problem:** organic growth is retail. You buy customers one at a time at full price, with your own cash and your own hours, so growth is capped by your time and your bank balance.
9. **Unique mechanism of the function:** buy customers wholesale. Acquire a whole business (customers, team, cash flow) and pay for it from its own profit instead of your capital.
10. **Unique mechanism of the solution:** the self-funding acquisition. Bank debt against the target's cash flow, seller finance and seller rollover close the gap, and the 1.5x cover test makes the business carry its own debt. Run it on a system so the second business is easier than the first.
11. **Conditions:** why owners can't just do it alone: listed deals are auctions; banks reject structures, not buyers; sellers sell to whoever makes them feel safe; and acquirers who get structure or site management wrong lose money (the healthcare group with ten unprofitable deals †).

### Sale 3 · Value
12. **USP:** G&L is run by people who did it from nothing: Josh's first two deals were 100% seller financed, and he built a multi seven-figure group on them. Sourcing, structure, finance and running the sites, under one roof.
13. **Features → levers:**
    - *Odds:* the 1.5x test before every offer; walk away from deals that fail it.
    - *Dream:* a group of businesses that run without you; an exit at a group multiple (illustrative).
    - *Effort:* you already know how to run the asset; structure does the heavy lifting.
    - *Time:* growth in one settlement instead of years.
14. **Anchoring:** what not doing it costs: another year buying customers at retail, the Saturdays, the competitor who buys the business you wanted.

### Sale 4 · Impulse
15. **Risk reversal:** a deal that fails the 1.5x test doesn't get done; the bigger risk is staying a single business while others consolidate.
16. **What they gain now:** first call on businesses that will never list, while the owners are deciding.
17. **What they lose by waiting:** the business they wanted, bought by someone else. Another year on the plateau.

**Close:** apply; if it's a fit, the team books a strategy call. $1M+ only, said plainly.
