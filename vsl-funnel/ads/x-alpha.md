# X "free alpha" posts

"Alpha" on X = an actionable edge given away like insider knowledge ("haven't shared this before, but here goes"), delivered with conviction by someone who has done it. Every tactic below is Josh's own, from his long-form videos, short clips or X posts. Written in the aggressive Yegor voice. † = Josh to confirm he's happy to say it publicly.

## X1 · Never mention the interest rate

Free alpha for anyone writing their first seller-finance offer:

Don't mention interest

At all

Most buyers send "I'll pay $X over 7 years at 6%" and then wonder why the seller's accountant rips it apart

Your first offer is just the term: "10 years, paid monthly." That's it

If the seller asks about interest, now you negotiate. Most never ask, because they're thinking about the total they'll walk away with, not the rate

On my first deal I agreed 7 years at 4%. Looking back I'm convinced I could have done it at zero

Always start at zero. You can only ever go up from there

## X2 · The one-line pitch

Haven't shared this before, but people keep asking what I actually say to a seller

Here it is, word for word:

"Nobody is going to pay you the price you want in cash. I'll pay you the price you want, plus more, for the privilege of paying it over time"

That's the whole pitch

Every owner who's been trying to sell for a while has already met the cash buyers. They lowballed him. They wanted him to stay on for five years. They wanted a discount for "risk"

You're the first person offering him his number

The ones who've been through a failed sale process get it instantly, because they've learned the hard way that something over time beats nothing at all

That sentence got me two 100% seller-financed deals with no personal guarantee and no security

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "PITCH"

## X3 · One cold email to reception

My first acquisition started with one cold email

Not to the owner. To the front desk

Reception forwarded it to the owner, and he called me back himself: "Got your email. I'm interested. What's the go?"

I gave him a one-liner, we were in business, and a few months later it closed 100% vendor finance

Here's the alpha nobody tells you about deal sourcing:

The best businesses never reach a broker. The owner is tired, he's never told anyone he'd sell, and the only thing stopping a conversation is that nobody asked

Brokers sell to the highest bidder. The front desk sells to whoever bothered to write

I made about a thousand calls before that deal. Four died on the table. It only takes one

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "SOURCE"

## X4 · Look 50km down the road

The fastest way to add millions to your business is sitting 25 to 50 km away

Free alpha: open Google, type your industry plus "near me" for the next town over, and you'll get a list of 100 businesses

One of them is the kingpin of that town. Established, profitable, an owner who's been doing it for 20 years and is tired

Call them. All of them. It takes one

You skip the hardest part of any business, getting your first hundred clients, because he already has them. You skip hiring, because he already has the team. You skip the brand, because the whole town already knows it

Plumber, accountant, physio, dog walker: it doesn't matter. The playbook is the same

Most owners are too proud to ask a competitor if he'd sell. That pride costs them a whole company's revenue every year

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "KINGPIN"

## X5 · If owners won't talk, change the industry

The first industry I tried to buy into was GP medical centres

Melbourne was in the longest lockdown in the world. The GPs were running testing sites, stressed out of their minds, half of them not taking new patients. Not one of them wanted to talk about selling

So I pivoted to allied health, physiotherapy

Overnight the conversations changed. Owners picked up the phone. They were receptive. It was easier to start every single conversation

Here's the alpha: if you're getting nothing but silence, you're not bad at sourcing. You're fishing in the wrong pond

Industries have moods. Pick one where the owners are tired, profitable and open, not one where they're fighting a fire

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "INDUSTRY"

## X6 · Banks want the big loan †

Free alpha about banks that took me years to understand

It is easier to get a banker to lend you $100 million than $1 million

Every banker has a quota and a bonus attached to it. Your little $500k loan doesn't move his number. A multi-million dollar facility against a profitable acquisition does

So stop walking in asking "how much can you give me?" That's exactly how you end up handing over your guarantees, your personal assets and your house

Walk in with the deal already structured: the target's profit covers the repayments one and a half times, the seller is financing part of it, and you have a board behind you

Now you're not asking for money. You're the deal he needs to hit his quarter

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "BANK"

## X7 · Anatomy of a 100% seller-finance deal

Everyone talks about seller finance. Almost nobody shows the clauses

Here's my first deal, line by line, and why each one matters:

100% seller financed: none of my money in the price

7 years at 4%: long enough that the repayments are small against the profit

9-month payment holiday: every dollar of profit stays in the business while I take over and fix what needs fixing

No personal guarantee and no security: if it goes wrong, my house isn't in the conversation

No bond, rent paid in arrears: the lease doesn't suck cash out on day one either

Result: about $150k in distributions in year one, and revenue has roughly tripled since

My second deal was 36 months with staged balloons and 6 months interest-only. Different clauses, same principle: protect the cash flow while you take over

You don't win a deal on price. You win it on the clauses

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "CLAUSES"

## X8 · Build the board before the deal

The cheapest unfair advantage in acquisitions costs you almost nothing

A board

Before my first deal I recruited one off LinkedIn: people who'd run healthcare businesses, including a director who'd built fitness businesses to eight figures and owned a physio clinic himself

I can teach anyone how to buy a company. That process is the same everywhere

What I can't know is the nuance of your industry, your town and your jurisdiction. What the regulator is thinking. What tax changes are coming. Which landlord is a nightmare

That's what the board is for. Instead of paying an accountant by the hour who might not know the answer, you've got people in your corner who've lived it

And sellers notice. A 25-year-old alone is a risk. A 25-year-old with a board of operators behind him is a serious buyer

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "BOARD"

## X9 · Everyone hates private equity. Use it

Every time private equity buys another vet clinic, dental practice or childcare centre, half the internet loses its mind

Here's the alpha: the sellers hate it too

A retiring owner doesn't want his staff of 20 years handed to a fund in New York that'll cut costs to hit a return target. He wants to know they'll be looked after and that his name will mean something after he's gone

That's the gap

An owner-operator buyer who keeps the staff, keeps the name and pays him his price over time beats a PE cheque more often than you'd think, because you're answering the questions PE never asks

I've done it twice, both 100% seller financed. Neither seller was choosing between me and nothing. They were choosing who they trusted with what they built

Doing $1M+ a year? DM me "NOTPE"
