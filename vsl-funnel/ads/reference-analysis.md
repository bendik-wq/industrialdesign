## A — `psywar BO vsl` (0:57)

**Structure**
- 0:00–0:21 **Borrowed tension.** A Russian-roulette interrogation scene from a war video game,
  with native dialogue and subtitles ("One chance, Mason", "You can't kill ME!"). Brand title
  "Psywar / psychological…" ghosted over the first two seconds. No cuts for 21 s. The game's own
  drama holds attention.
- 0:20 Lower-third whisper text "in vsls" lands *on the climax*, the gunshot/"Fuck!" beat.
- 0:21–0:48 **Montage of mechanism.** About 30 hard cuts in 27 s (one every ~0.9 s). EEG headsets,
  polygraph paper, "DETERMINISTIC PATHWAY ANALYSIS" screen, wireframe brains, Matrix code,
  CIA-style lab footage. Text overlays one or two words at a time: "VSLs" → "evolutionary
  psychology" → "psyops lvl tech" → "Psywar for marketing" → "NEURO DATA X MARKETING".
- 0:48–0:57 **Logo hold.** Slow push-ins on a man in an electrode helmet. "psywar.ai" watermark,
  no CTA.

**Visual**: everything is graded to one magenta/violet LUT (≈ #6A1BFF → #C21BFF), with crushed
blacks, VHS grain, scanlines and slight chromatic aberration. Serif text (Times-like) at 40–60%
opacity, centred, in the same purple, so it reads as *watermark*, not caption.

**Sound**: 0–21 s is in-game dialogue (voice band 85–99%, sub ~0). At 0:22 a sub-bass drone takes
over (sub energy 25–40%) and stays flat, a constant dread bed. The last 8 s are sub only (voice
band < 10%), so the logo sits on rumble. No voiceover.

**Storytelling move**: *Borrowed frame.* The viewer is already inside a life-or-death scene they
recognise, and the brand claims that tension: "this is what we do to buyers."

**Tonality**: menacing, insider, "forbidden knowledge". Classified, not salesy.

**Weakness for paid use**: copyrighted game footage (Meta IP takedown risk), no offer, no CTA,
and the 4:3 frame wastes feed real estate.

## B — `christmas` (1:11)

**Structure** (on-screen poem, word-by-word reveals)
1. 0:00–0:06 Ops room (operator at CRT monitors) → **Psywar.ai / Psychological operations
   & Intelligence Service** crest card → figure with a glowing spine and energy wings.
2. 0:08–0:11 **Strobe burst.** About 20 cuts in 2.5 s (every 0.1–0.2 s) of eye and organic textures.
   This is the only fast section, a deliberate "glitch" spike.
3. 0:12–0:30 Monospace typewriter text on near-black: "Psywar.ai — A dream" → "A dream that
   wouldn't end" → "over" → "I repeated" → "every mistake" → "but before I knew it" →
   "I lost count…"
4. 0:32–0:35 "**over 100 million times**" over neon blood-vessel/neural macro.
5. 0:36–0:47 "And" → "I wanted to reach…" → "I wanted" → "to protect…"
6. 0:48–0:53 Physical props: a **C.I.A. SECRET FILES** box and an **ARMY**-stamped dossier
   with the Psywar seal. The tangible artefact proves the claim.
7. 0:54–1:11 Slow drift through a neural-network starfield and an ops table with holographic
   globe: "**we help you win / when no one else can**". Fade to black.

**Visual**: same purple LUT but darker, with teal/green accents introduced in the dream
corridor, giving a two-tone palette (violet + toxic teal). Long, slow handheld drifts. Text is
low-contrast, small and centred, so you lean in to read it. Retention comes from *effort*.

**Sound**: builds like a trailer. 0–8 s quiet (-20 dB), the strobe hits a sub spike (73% sub at
0:08), then a steady climb to the peak at 0:33–0:55 (-9 dB, sub 45–72%) under "100 million
times" and the CIA files, and an outro decrescendo to -23 dB. The music *is* the arc.

**Storytelling move**: first-person confession (a "dream" of repeating mistakes 100M times,
which reads as simulated buyer journeys), turned into a mission ("to protect…"), then the promise.
Sultanic would call it a *mechanism origin story told as myth*.

**Tonality**: melancholic, cinematic, mythic. The brand speaks like a veteran, not a vendor.

**Tonality**: conspiratorial, authoritative, cold. "We have the files."

## D — Ecom University "Zone of Genius" Instagram ad (3:00) ★ strongest template

This is the one to model for paid. It is a **long-form text-led story ad** that holds for
3 minutes on Instagram.

Large serif *title cards* on black (centred, one phrase at a time), and small monospace

### Mechanics that make it work
1. **Open loop with a math hook.** "Odds of dying?" → "1 in 1" → "0". A number is a
   pattern interrupt, and the morbid question stops the thumb. The loop closes only at 2:48
   ("odds of you *really living*?"). **Bookend structure.**
2. **Sentence fragmentation.** Title cards split one sentence across 3–4 cards
   ("The problem" / "you just can't" / "see it"). Each card is a micro open loop, so you must wait
   for the next one. Pace: about 1 card per 1.5 s.
3. **One controlling metaphor** (fingerprint → internal fingerprint → Zone of Genius), repeated
   visually at least 8 times and morphing (fingerprint → Earth → galaxy). The named concept is
   delivered at 1:04, about 35% in, *after* the desire has been built.
4. **Unseen-but-felt proof** ("nobody has seen the wind") carries the claim with no evidence
   needed. An analogy instead of a testimonial.
5. **Enemy / villain** at 1:51: "Then the world changed", the school system. The 80s-film
   look, an F grade and a disappointed father give the viewer *their own* backstory without
   targeting them ("You may have even believed it" invites, it doesn't accuse; Meta-safe).
6. **Historical authority** (Ancient Greece) reframes the offer as *recovering something lost*,
   not learning something new. That is a classic "old secret" reframe.
7. **Unfinished sentence** "It made others think you were… A" lets the viewer complete it.
8. **Desire montage** of recognisable outcomes ("the phone in your hand", "the song you can't
   stop playing"). Note: these use celebrity and film clips, which we **cannot** replicate.
9. **CTA is soft and identity-based**: "Find your Zone of Genius." + URL on a white card. It's
   the first white frame in 3 minutes, so the colour flip itself is the CTA signal.

### Visual system
- 16:9 content in a 9:16 feed (letterboxed). Black bars are part of the look and leave room
  for UI.
- Two type systems: **condensed high-contrast serif** (Didone-ish) for title cards,
  **monospace** (Courier-style) for subtitles. Elegant plus documentary.
- Palette: pure black, warm amber/gold (Greece, desk lamps), cool blue (Earth, eyes). No
  brand colour until the white end card.
- Edit rhythm: 1.5–3 s average shot, crossfades in the cosmic section, hard cuts in the
  school section, and a 0.5 s black frame before each act change ("Then the world changed").
- Almost entirely **AI-generated cinematic B-roll** (Greek scenes, 80s kid, eyes) plus stock
  cosmos. Very producible for us.

| Authority | game | CIA files | archive general | **Ancient Greece** | historical deal-making precedent |
| Palette | violet LUT | violet + teal | cobalt | **black / amber / blue** | dark, cinematic, one accent |
| Type | ghost serif | mono typewriter | small sans | **serif cards + mono subs** | copy D |
| Audio | drone | trailer build | VO + bed | VO + score (assumed) | ElevenLabs VO or Josh VO + score |
| CTA | none | none | none | **white identity card + URL** | white card, "See if your business qualifies" |

**Recommendation**: build G&L ads on **D's structure** (bookended math hook → fragmented title
cards → one metaphor → villain → historical authority → desire montage → white-card CTA), and use
**C's "named mechanism" captions** for credibility. Treat A and B as mood references only (palette,
grain, strobe spikes). Don't copy their copyrighted footage or the conspiracy framing.

## Compliance flags spotted in the references (do not replicate)

  and film/TV clips (Meta IP + right of publicity).
- C's "subjugate", MKUltra/LSD imagery would trip Meta review for a financial/advisory advertiser.
