# Five belief-shift scripts (Opus)

Written from `ads/brief.md`: each script makes the four sales in order (content, hope, value, impulse), one segment and one concept per ad. Section markers (▸) are for the editor and aren't read aloud. One line is one breath; each runs 2½ to 3 minutes spoken at the narrator's pace (the Zone of Genius reference ran 3).

**Proof:** Josh's approved facts and one member result, plus two items from Bendik marked † that need sign-off before anything runs (Josh's first two deals having no personal guarantee and no security; the healthcare group owner with ten unprofitable acquisitions).

---

## 1. The Hidden Door

*Segment: owners on a plateau, paying more every year for ads, agencies and hires. Controlling image: two doors.*

▸ CONTENT

Every owner you know is queuing at the same door.

The sign above it says growth.

Behind it sits an agency on a monthly retainer, a recruiter with a placement fee, and a new sales hire who needs six months to find their feet.

▸ HOPE

You've been through that door, and you know what it costs.

Every year the ads get dearer, the good hires get harder to find, and each new customer costs more than the last.

That isn't bad luck. It's how that door works.

Growing one customer at a time is buying at retail.

You pay full price for every customer, with your own cash and your own hours, so your growth can never outrun your time or your bank balance.

Further down the corridor there's a second door.

There's no sign on it, and nobody is waiting.

Behind it is a business that already has the customers you're paying to chase.

The team is hired and the phone already rings, and the owner is getting ready to stop.

That's buying at wholesale. A whole customer base, a team and a cash flow, in one transaction.

It's how the largest companies on earth grow. Disney bought Marvel instead of inventing a thousand heroes, and Facebook bought Instagram instead of trying to beat it.

And nobody tells business owners that the same door is open to them.

The bank lends against the cash flow of the business you buy.

The seller finances part of the price and rolls part of it over to stay in the deal.

When that business's profit covers the repayments one and a half times, it pays for itself.

▸ VALUE

Josh Li walked through that door at twenty-five, unemployed, with nothing.

His first two deals were one hundred percent seller financed. †No personal guarantee, and no security.

He built a multi seven-figure healthcare group on them, and now he shows owners how to do the same.

▸ IMPULSE

Every month you stay at the first door, you pay retail for growth.

And the business behind the second door goes to whoever knocks first.

This is for owners of profitable businesses doing between one and a hundred million dollars a year.

Apply below, and our team books your strategy call if it's a fit.

Smaller than that? Grow the core business first. This isn't for you yet.

## 2. A Thousand Calls

*Segment: owners who believe buying a business takes millions in cash. Controlling image: the call log.*

▸ CONTENT

Josh Li was twenty-five and unemployed.

He had no business, no investors and no experience owning anything.

So he picked up the phone and called business owners, about a thousand of them.

Four deals got close and died on the table.

The fifth one closed.

▸ HOPE

You've been told that buying a business takes millions in the bank.

So you've never seriously looked, and every year you grow the slow way instead.

That belief is the most expensive thing you own.

Josh's seller financed one hundred percent of the price, over seven years, at four percent.

†There was no personal guarantee and no security.

He put in none of his own money, and on day one he owned a healthcare business with about thirteen thousand customers.

In the first year it paid him about a hundred and fifty thousand dollars.

He was the youngest person in Australian history to do a fully seller financed healthcare deal.

Luck had nothing to do with it. He used a structure.

The seller is paid over time, out of the profits of the business they sold.

A bank lends against the target's cash flow, never your savings.

Seller rollover keeps the seller in the deal with a slice of what comes next.

Build it so the profit covers the repayments one and a half times, and the business carries its own debt.

Build it well, and some deals are financed above the price, leaving working capital in the business.

One member closed a two point four million dollar deal financed above its price. That's their result, not a promise of yours.

▸ VALUE

The hard part was never the money.

It's knowing which deals pass the test, which sellers will finance, and how to make a lender say yes.

That's what Josh learned across a thousand calls, and what his team now does with owners.

▸ IMPULSE

Now look at where you're starting.

You already run a profitable business doing a million dollars a year or more.

You have a track record a lender can read, a team that can run without you for a week, and credit you've never pointed at an acquisition.

Josh started with a phone and a list. You're starting with all of that.

Apply below, and our team books your strategy call if it's a fit.

Smaller than that? Grow the core business first. This isn't for you yet.

## 3. The Second One Is Easier

*Segment: owners who fear a second business would break them. Controlling image: the second set of keys.*

▸ CONTENT

The first business you ran nearly broke you.

You learned payroll by getting it wrong, and hiring by hiring the wrong person first.

You learned what a bad month feels like at two in the morning, with the bank balance open on your phone.

▸ HOPE

So when someone says "buy a second business", your body answers before your head does.

Twice the hours, twice the stress, twice the risk.

That fear is built on a wrong assumption.

The pain of the first business was tuition.

You were building the machine while running it, with no systems, no managers and no margin for error.

The second business doesn't need any of that built again.

It inherits your systems, your suppliers, your finance team and the manager you'd trust with the keys.

Your buying power goes up the day it settles, and your overheads get spread across two sets of revenue.

The machine you spent a decade building finally gets a second engine.

And each acquisition after that gets easier, because the machine already exists.

▸ VALUE

You don't need to learn how to run a business. You already know how.

What nobody taught you is how to buy one so that it pays for itself.

The bank lends against the target's own cash flow.

The seller finances part of the price and rolls part of it over.

When the profit covers the repayments one and a half times, the business carries its own debt.

Josh Li bought his first two companies this way, both one hundred percent seller financed, and built a multi seven-figure healthcare group on them.

▸ IMPULSE

The owners who build groups aren't tougher than you.

They worked out that the hardest business they'll ever run is the first one, and you've already done that.

Every year you wait, you run one engine at full throttle while the people around you add a second.

If you run a profitable business doing a million dollars a year or more, apply below, and our team books your strategy call if it's a fit.

Smaller than that? Grow the core business first. This isn't for you yet.

## 4. Ten Deals, No Profit

*Segment: owners already doing acquisitions that disappoint. Controlling image: ten lines in red.*

▸ CONTENT

We sat down with the owner of a healthcare group running dozens of sites.†

From the outside it looked like a machine.

A recognised brand, serious revenue, and a reputation as the buyer in the region.

Then the owner showed us the number.

The group's last ten acquisitions were unprofitable.

Ten businesses bought, ten sets of keys collected, and every one of them a line in red.

▸ HOPE

If you've done deals yourself, you've felt a version of this.

The numbers look right at signing, and twelve months later the business is costing you money.

Most owners blame the deal, or the market, or the manager they put in.

Two things broke those ten deals, and neither of them was the price.

The structure was wrong. The debt was sized to the purchase instead of the profit, so the target couldn't carry it and nothing was left over.

And the sites were run the old way, one manager at a time, with the owner as the bottleneck for every decision.

Those are the two mistakes that sink acquirers at every size, and nobody teaches the fix.

▸ VALUE

Done right, a deal is built to carry itself from the first month.

The debt is sized to cash flow at one and a half times cover, before the offer goes in.

Seller finance and seller rollover close the gap, so the seller stays invested in the handover.

And every site runs on one operating system, without you in the room.

Josh Li built his healthcare group on exactly those two disciplines, starting with two acquisitions that were both one hundred percent seller financed.

▸ IMPULSE

A deal that fails the test never gets signed. That's the risk reversal built into the method.

The real risk is the next deal you do the old way.

If you run a seven or eight figure business and you want your next ten acquisitions to make money, apply below, and our team books your strategy call if it's a fit.

Under a million a year? This isn't for you yet.

## 5. The Map

*Segment: owners expanding into new cities, states or countries. Controlling image: one pin versus the whole map.*

▸ CONTENT

Every new market charges the same entry fee.

A brand nobody there has heard of, a team that has never worked together, and customers who already have a supplier they trust.

▸ HOPE

Most companies pay it.

They open a branch, hire locally, put one pin in the map and wait for it to take.

Three years later they're still the newcomer, and you know because you've watched it happen, maybe to you.

The slow part was never your product. It's trust, and trust can't be built quickly.

The fastest companies don't build it. They buy it.

They find the business that already owns that market, and they acquire it.

The customers come with it, and so do the licences, the local relationships and the name people already trust.

A new city, a new state or a new country becomes yours in one settlement.

And if you already operate across borders, it's the fastest way to plant a flag anywhere you choose.

▸ VALUE

It doesn't take a war chest.

The bank lends against the cash flow of the business you buy.

The seller finances part of the price and rolls part of it over, so they stay invested in a clean handover.

When that business's profit covers the repayments one and a half times, the new market pays for its own entry.

Josh Li built a multi seven-figure healthcare group this way, starting with two deals that were both one hundred percent seller financed.

▸ IMPULSE

Your competitors are still pushing pins into the map.

The business that already owns your next market will be bought by someone. The only question is whether it's you.

If you run a profitable business doing between one and a hundred million dollars a year, apply below, and our team books your strategy call if it's a fit.

Smaller than that? Grow the core business first. This isn't for you yet.
