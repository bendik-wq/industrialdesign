# Desire doctrine: how the masterclass sells the buyer's path

The masterclass (`scripts/ai-webinar/script-ai-josh.md`, and `script-ai.md` generated from it) and its keynote deck
(`public/assets/deck/`) sell one action: **apply, then book a strategy call with Josh's team.** This page is the
doctrine behind every emotional beat: what the viewer should feel, minute by minute, which device produces the feeling,
what the slide does about it, and where the line sits between persuasion and manipulation.

It is our own distillation of three sources, written in our own words:
- a third-party study of emotion in short video sales letters ("Psywar: emotion in VSLs"; not kept in this repo),
- Gary Bencivenga's method (the public *Bencivenga Bullets* and *Marketing Maxims*: proof over promise, the
  persuasion equation, reasons why), as scored with the house's 115-check audit,
- the house direct-response rules (pain and what's in it for them first, one big idea, specificity, emotion then logic,
  story, the boldest claim the proof can carry, future pacing as a picture, objections answered, one call to action)
  and the house evidence-and-ethics rules for persuasion techniques.

## Ten laws

1. **Feeling first, reasons right behind it.** Every peak is a scene the viewer can see, and the logic that lets them
   justify the feeling arrives within the same slide or the next one. A feeling with no reason is manipulation; a reason
   with no feeling is a lecture.
2. **Show the moment, never the adjective.** One object, one sound, one exact time per scene: the tea gone cold with the
   bag still in it, the dishwasher ticking as it cools, the phone buzzing on the tablecloth. Concrete detail is what
   makes a story believable and a future wantable.
3. **Sell the reach, not only the arrival.** Wanting runs on anticipation. The felt future always ends on the next thing
   Dan is reaching for (the call he's looking forward to making, the second deal), not on a finished life.
4. **Put the two states edge to edge.** Before and after, path one and path two, built object for object (the same sign,
   the same phone, the same question at Easter) so the gap argues by itself and no sentence has to explain it.
5. **Make the cost physical and countable.** "I work weekends" becomes seventy-eight squares on a slide. "Waiting" becomes
   fifty-two Saturdays, twelve month-end reports and one more year on every owner with no successor.
6. **Join every claim to its proof at the hip.** The strongest true proof (two acquisitions, both seller financed in full)
   is on screen in the first two minutes, again with the photograph of a signing, again when the viewer asks "how do I
   know", and again at the close. Never a claim bigger than its proof.
7. **Confess before you promise.** Credibility comes from candour: Dan's "I'll see" said to his daughter, the deals that
   fail the test, what the guarantee does not promise, who this is not for.
8. **Weld beats by cause.** Each beat happens *because* of the last one (the call from Graham, so the drive past the yard,
   so the sum on the back of the report), never "and then". A flat stretch is a beat with no stimulus: find it and fix it.
9. **Ask at the peak, at the peak's temperature.** The call to apply lands right after a desire peak, quiet when the scene
   was quiet ("If there's a question at your own kitchen table that you keep answering with 'I'll see'..."), with a
   visible no every time.
10. **Close the loop you opened.** The opening question, the seller's question and Dan's Tuesday night are each paid off,
    and the close repeats the promise *with* its proof, the three reasons and the guarantee next to the last ask.

## The devices, and where they live
- **Sensory specifics:** one physical anchor per scene, in the present tense, with an exact time and place on the slide's
  slug ("Illustrative · Wednesday, 7:10 am · The kitchen").
- **Motifs that carry feeling across the hour:** the phone (buzzing on the tablecloth, in the jacket pocket, the one
  making the calls), the kitchen table at 9:40 pm (cold tea, then hot tea), the sign over Graham's gate (faded, a
  stranger's name, a second name under his), and Easter ("I'll see", then "We're going").
- **Future pacing:** always labelled illustrative, a picture and not a forecast, built only from what the structure and
  the engagement involve (a manager in each site, the seller's handover, the note and the rollover). No income, no
  timeline promised.
- **Contrast:** pain scenes are near-black, the wanted future is warm white; the two paths share one slide, split down the
  middle, object for object.
- **Open loops:** three nested loops (Dan's Tuesday night, the big domino, the seller's question), opened in the first ten
  minutes and closed in reverse; the proof slide adds a fourth ("it wasn't luck, and it wasn't money").
- **Proof adjacency:** only the approved facts (WEBINAR-NOTES.md, "Proof used"), on screen at the start, at the button,
  at "how do I know" and at the close.
- **The felt future in the deck:** one moment per click, earlier lines dimming as the next arrives, so the presenter can
  hold each beat as long as it needs.

## The ethics line
- Persuasion, never deception. Every technique here must still work if the viewer knew it was being used.
- No fabricated proof: no invented clients, testimonials, numbers, logos or quotes. Composites (Dan, Graham, Priya, Tom,
  Leah, Mark) are said to be composites, on screen and in the words, every time.
- Illustrative numbers are labelled illustrative on the same slide; the copy audit fails any number that isn't.
- Whenever the funding of a deal is described, the seller rollover is named with the lender and the seller's note.
- No fake scarcity, no timers, no "today only": the only limit spoken is the real one (a limited number of engagements
  per intake, a number only if the setting exists).
- No hidden "no": every ask keeps a visible way out, and the close says plainly that closing the tab is a fair choice.
- No covert or "below awareness" techniques: no flashed images, no trance language, no chanting. The identity line is
  said once, quietly, by the viewer to themselves, and tied to a behaviour (noticing, then applying).
- The call is never called free; the engagement's fee exists and is covered on the call; the guarantee is read in full
  with its conditions and what it does not promise.
- Owners under money stress are a vulnerable audience: pain is ordinary and true, never catastrophic, and every loss is
  followed within a slide by a doable next step.

## The scorecard
Score each criterion 0 (absent) to 3 (excellent) on the whole script, and the per-slide version on every beat:

1. Curiosity and the master loop: opened early, dosed, closed at the ask.
2. Sensory scenes at every emotional peak (objects, sounds, an exact time and place).
3. The felt future: the viewer steps into the life after, and the reach toward the next thing.
4. Contrast: two states edge to edge.
5. The cost of waiting, physical and true.
6. Aspiration: a person the viewer wants to become, embodied in a scene.
7. Relief: the mechanism as the missing piece; not your fault; risk handled.
8. Pride and identity: a new identity worth claiming, tied to a behaviour.
9. Proof adjacency: each big claim joined to a true proof in the same breath.
10. Believability: candour, limits, illustrative labels, "yeah, sure" answered first.
11. Arousal curve and causal weld: no flat stretch; beats joined by cause.
12. Calls to action: at the peaks, at the peak's temperature, with a visible no, closing the loop.

How it is run: a fast model scores every slide first (desire 0 to 10 and eight sub-scores) and the whole script against
the scorecard and the Bencivenga 115-check audit; a person reads every flag and decides. The model's lines are drafts
for a person to judge, never shipped as written. Then `npx vite-node scripts/ai-webinar/audit-script.ts` and
`present/audit-deck.ts` must print 0 flags.
