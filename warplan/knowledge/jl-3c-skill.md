
# 3C Acquisition Model

A systematic framework for acquiring established businesses with minimal or no upfront personal capital through creative financing and institutional credibility.

## Core Philosophy

The concept of no-money-down business buying is not original—just unconventional. Success requires leveraging other people's experience (elite advisors) and other people's money (commercial debt, vendor financing) rather than personal capital.

## The 3C Framework

### C1: Capabilities (Building Institutional Credibility)

**Objective**: Build credibility that unlocks lenders, sellers, and advisors without personal cash.

#### Internal Team Structure

Assemble a board/advisory team with:
- **Chair**: Former executive or industry titan for credibility
- **Finance Lead**: CFO/credit veteran who understands deal structures
- **Legal Lead**: M&A attorney or former general counsel
- **Sector Operator**: Industry expert with operational experience

**Recruitment Strategy**:
- Offer 2%-10% founders' equity instead of cash
- Target recently retired executives looking for meaningful involvement
- Use LinkedIn, direct outreach, and industry networks
- Pitch the vision and ground-floor opportunity
- Structure vesting tied to signed SPA and 12-month post-close support

#### External Professional Bench

Secure top-tier service providers:
- **Accountants**: Big Four or quality regional firms for Quality of Earnings (QoE)
- **Lawyers**: Specialized M&A counsel for SPA/APA, employment, leases
- **Industry SMEs**: Consultants specific to target sector

**Fee Structures**:
- Delayed/rolled fees tied to closing or post-close working capital
- "No money down" by deferring professional fees
- Working capital loan covers legal/accounting upon close

#### Credibility Outputs

Create professional artifacts:
- Board/advisory roster with bios and track records
- Advisor engagement terms documenting equity/scope
- Credibility packet: thesis memo + NBIO template + diligence checklist
- "Over 250 years cumulative experience, $300M+ in transactions" type positioning

**Key Principle**: Your team's credibility substitutes for personal capital and experience.

### C2: Capital (Engineering No-Money-Down Structures)

**Objective**: Fund acquisitions with minimal buyer cash while protecting debt service coverage.

#### Capital Structure Menu

**Primary Sources** (Preferred order):
1. **100% Vendor Finance**: 
   - Seller note covering full purchase price
   - Unsecured where negotiated
   - Payment holiday (6-12 months post-close)
   - Interest: 3-5% typical
   - Term: 5-7 years

2. **Blended Structures**:
   - 60% commercial debt + 40% seller rollover equity
   - Commercial debt provides validation
   - Seller retains skin in the game

3. **Working Capital Lines**:
   - Post-close loan/LOC for operations
   - Covers legal fees, integration, runway
   - Typically 10% of annual revenue
   - Drawn immediately at settlement

**Add-On Mechanisms**:
- Asset-Based Lending (ABL) on AR/inventory
- Mezzanine debt for larger deals
- Earn-outs tied to performance
- Rent deferrals when seller owns real estate

#### Banking Relationship Strategy

**Before Deal Sourcing**:
- Interview 50+ lenders across major banks and regional players
- Collect key parameters:
  - DSCR and ICR thresholds (target 1.5x minimum)
  - Sector appetite and ticket sizes
  - Amortization periods and balloon options
  - Centralized vs. branch decisioning
  - Collateral requirements
  - Personal Guarantee (PG) policies
  - Standard covenant packages

**Approach Banks as Interviewer, Not Beggar**:
- "I'm building relationships across the banking sector"
- Present with board credibility behind you
- Request term sheets for hypothetical scenarios
- Build rapport before needing money

#### Financial Modeling Requirements

Create bankable models showing:
- Base case, bear case, bull case scenarios
- Pro-forma DSCR ≥1.50x under base case
- Covenant headroom demonstrations
- Cash conversion cycle analysis
- Integration cost assumptions
- Conservative revenue/EBITDA adjustments

**Critical Metrics**:
- Maintain 1.5x Debt Service Coverage Ratio minimum
- Zero or minimal Personal Guarantees
- Maximize unsecured portions
- Align debt service holidays with integration plans

### C3: Closing (Process Control & Execution)

**Objective**: Control the process, compress timelines, eliminate deposits and broker friction.

#### Five-Step Deal Flow

**Step 1: Off-Market Origination**
- Direct-to-owner outreach (cold calls, emails, LinkedIn)
- Multi-channel approach: phone → email → LinkedIn → mail
- Golden question: "Have you ever thought about selling?"
- Target: 1,000 calls to fill pipeline
- Build relationships over time (may take 6-12 months)
- Avoid brokers to maximize leverage and minimize competition

**Step 2: Qualification Gate**
- NDA signature required
- Request: ≥3 years financial statements + tax returns
- For healthcare: billing breakdowns by practitioner
- If seller provides these, indicates genuine motivation
- Review with board/advisors before proceeding

**Step 3: Indicative Offer**
- Use NBIO (Non-Binding Indicative Offer) or LOI format
- Include:
  - Headline purchase price and valuation methodology
  - Proposed structure (vendor finance %, terms, holiday)
  - Key conditions (DD, financing approval, lease assignments, licenses)
  - Exclusivity period (typically 60-90 days)
  - Target timeline to close
- Front-load economics, leave details for later
- Aim to negotiate 80% of deal in LOI stage

**Step 4: Due Diligence**
- Formal legal, financial, operational review (90 days typical)
- **Primary Focus**: Quality of Earnings (QoE)
  - Revenue sustainability and customer concentration
  - EBITDA adjustments and normalization
  - Working capital requirements
  - Maintainability of cash flows post-acquisition
- Track issues in formal diligence log
- Use findings to negotiate price adjustments
- Board/external team conducts reviews

**Step 5: Contractual Close**
- Lawyers draft SPA (Share Purchase Agreement) or APA (Asset Purchase Agreement)
- Contract papering: 4 weeks target
- Maintain leadership pressure on lawyers to avoid fee-jacking
- Tight scope, clear timelines, prioritized issue lists
- Settlement: execute documents, transfer funds, obtain keys
- Immediate working capital draw to cover costs

#### Process Control Tactics

**Maintaining Leverage**:
- Run parallel pipelines (never single-thread)
- Establish clear walk-away criteria
- Don't over-negotiate minor points
- Be willing to say "no worries, good luck" when sellers bluff
- 90% of sellers claiming "other buyers" are bluffing

**Timeline Discipline**:
- DD window: 90 days maximum
- Contract papering: 4 weeks target
- Total process: 3-6 months from LOI to close

**Seller Management**:
- Keep seller involved 6 months to 3 years (negotiated transition)
- Interview staff before close to prevent exodus
- Clear continuity plan for customers/employees
- Structured exit for seller with accountability

## Red Flags to Avoid

Walk away or heavily discount when you see:

**Financial Red Flags**:
- Financials and tax returns don't match
- Slipping gross margin masked by accounting changes
- Underfunded working capital at close
- Customer/supplier concentration (1-2 entities = >30% revenue)
- Tax debt or liens

**Operational Red Flags**:
- Seller wants immediate exit with no transition
- Very high employee turnover
- Unassignable key customer contracts
- Regulatory non-compliance in licensed sectors

**Process Red Flags**:
- Seller unwillingness to explain details
- "Other buyers interested" claims (usually false)
- Pressure tactics or artificial urgency
- Broker-controlled process with inflated numbers

**Size Threshold**: Deals under $1M rarely worth the effort—insufficient cash flow to support you and debt service.

Every deal must satisfy:
- Financing approval (if using commercial debt)

- Lease assignments or renewals secured

- Key personnel employment agreements

## Industry Selection Criteria

**Target Characteristics**:
- Traditional, stable business models
- 15%+ net margins minimum
- Large, fragmented markets
- High boomer ownership (retiring sellers)
- Recurring revenue/repeat customers
- Asset-light preferred

**Strong Sectors**:
- Healthcare (GP clinics, allied health, dental)
- Trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical)
- Commercial services (cleaning, logistics)
- Professional services (accounting, legal)
- Waste management and environmental

**Avoid**:
- Highly commoditized low-margin businesses
- Technology-disrupted industries
- Heavy regulatory or capital requirements (unless you have expertise)
- Single-customer dependencies

## Roll-Up Strategy for Maximum Value

**The Monopoly Method**:

1. Pick fragmented vertical in large metro area
2. Build credible team first
3. Source off-market deals continuously
4. Structure using vendor finance + rollover + commercial debt
5. Team conducts due diligence
6. Present to financiers with board backing
7. Lawyers draft contracts
8. Incorporate and close
9. Return to Step 3 and repeat

**Exit Strategy**:
- Buy at 3-5x EBITDA (small business multiples)
- Sell combined platform at 8-12x EBITDA (corporate/PE multiples)
- 10 businesses = 1% market share → $5-10M exit
- 50 businesses = 5% market share → $40-80M exit  
- 100 businesses = 10% market share → $100-200M exit

**Time Horizon**: 3-10 years from first deal to exit

## Advanced Negotiation Tactics

### The "No Worries, Good Luck" Technique

**When to Use**: Seller claims they have other buyers or applies pressure tactics

**How It Works**:
1. Seller: "I have three other buyers interested at higher prices"
2. You: "No worries, good luck with those buyers"
3. Walk away confidently
4. 90% of the time, seller comes back within 2-7 days

**Psychology**: Calling the bluff demonstrates you have options and aren't desperate. This reestablishes your leverage position.

### The Payment Holiday Close

**Negotiation Flow**:
1. Agree on headline purchase price first
2. Then introduce: "Given integration risk, I'd like a 9-12 month payment holiday"
3. Frame as: "This gives me time to ensure the business maintains performance"
4. Offer slight interest rate increase if needed (e.g., 4% → 5%)
5. Emphasize: "You still get your full price, just delayed start"

**Why It Works**: Sellers focus on headline price. Once that's agreed, structural terms become secondary. The payment holiday gives you crucial runway without touching your cash.

### The Unsecured Conversion

**Standard Ask**: Bank or seller wants security over assets

**Counter-Approach**:
1. "Given my board's track record and our operational plan..."
2. "We'd prefer to keep this unsecured to maintain flexibility"
3. "However, we're happy to provide detailed monthly reporting"
4. "And maintain a cash reserve covenant of $X"

**Fallback Positions** (in order):
1. Security only on specific assets (not blanket lien)
2. Security with higher debt service buffer (1.75x vs 1.5x DSCR)
3. Security with subordination agreement if bringing in additional capital later
4. Partial security (e.g., on equipment but not AR/inventory)

### The Founder Equity Offer (Board Recruitment)

**Framework**:

  - 50% on first signed SPA

## Seller Psychology Mastery

### Motivation Hierarchy

**Tier 1 - Immediate Sellers** (Close in 30-90 days):
- Health crisis (cancer, heart attack)
- Divorce or family emergency
- Burnout after 20-30 years
- Partnership dispute requiring exit
- Regulatory pressure they can't handle

**Tier 2 - Near-Term Sellers** (Close in 3-12 months):
- Retirement planning (boomer demographic)
- Want to travel/relocate
- Tired of day-to-day operations
- See industry headwinds coming
- Kids don't want the business

**Tier 3 - Future Sellers** (Nurture 12+ months):
- "Just curious about value"
- Successful but exploring options
- Considering next chapter
- Building relationship with potential buyers

### Reading Seller Signals

**High Motivation Indicators**:
- Provides financials within 1-2 days of NDA
- Responds to emails within hours
- Asks about your timeline
- Discusses personal plans post-sale
- Introduces you to key staff early
- Flexible on structure discussions

**Low Motivation Indicators**:
- Takes weeks to provide basic info
- Constantly reschedules meetings
- Non-responsive to questions
- Focuses only on price, ignores structure
- Hasn't thought through post-sale plans
- Defensive about business issues

### The Relationship-Building Cadence

**Month 1**: Initial cold outreach, NDA, basic financials review
**Month 2-3**: If not motivated, back off. Send occasional industry articles
**Month 4-6**: "Just checking in" email every 6-8 weeks
**Month 7-12**: Quarterly touchpoint. Position as industry relationship, not just buyer
**Month 13+**: Annual check-in. Often this is when life events create motivation

**Script Evolution**:
- First call: "Have you thought about selling?"
- Follow-up 1: "Just wanted to stay in touch"
- Follow-up 2: "Saw [industry news], made me think of you"
- Follow-up 3: "Still building in [area], keeping you in mind"
- Follow-up 4: "How's business? Any changes in your thinking?"

- Customer concentration analysis
- Revenue by customer segment
- Customer retention rates
- Average customer lifetime value
- One-time vs recurring revenue split
- Seasonal revenue patterns
- Payment terms and AR aging

**EBITDA Adjustments**:
- Owner compensation normalization
- One-time expenses removal

**Working Capital Assessment**:
- Current vs historical working capital needs
- Cash conversion cycle
- Inventory turns
- AR collection periods
- AP payment terms
- Required cash balance for operations

**Post-Acquisition Sustainability**:
- Revenue dependencies on seller relationships
- Staff retention risk
- Customer transition plan
- Supplier relationship dependencies
- Contract assignability

## Common Pitfalls and Solutions

### Pitfall 1: Analysis Paralysis
**Problem**: Spending months researching instead of calling
**Solution**: Set action quota (50 calls/week minimum) before allowing more research

### Pitfall 2: First Deal Syndrome  
**Problem**: Trying to close the first deal you find, even if it's bad
**Solution**: Maintain pipeline of 10+ opportunities. Walk away from bad deals.

### Pitfall 3: Broker Dependence
**Problem**: Only looking at broker listings
**Solution**: 80% of activity should be direct outreach, 20% can be broker deals

### Pitfall 4: Weak Board
**Problem**: Friends and family as board vs. real operators
**Solution**: If they don't have relevant exits/deal experience, they shouldn't be on board

### Pitfall 5: Undercapitalized Close
**Problem**: Not securing working capital line before close
**Solution**: Working capital line must be approved and available at settlement

### Pitfall 6: Seller Exit Too Fast
**Problem**: Seller leaves immediately, business suffers
**Solution**: Minimum 6-month transition with structured exit plan and milestones

### Pitfall 7: Staff Exodus
**Problem**: Key employees leave after acquisition
**Solution**: Meet all staff pre-close. Offer retention bonuses for key people (90 days + 1 year).

### Pitfall 8: Over-Leverage
**Problem**: DSCR below 1.3x, no cushion for problems
**Solution**: Never close with DSCR below 1.5x. Build in conservatism.

### Pitfall 9: No Exit Plan
**Problem**: Buying businesses without knowing how you'll exit
**Solution**: Have roll-up thesis and exit strategy before first acquisition

### Pitfall 10: Going It Alone
**Problem**: Trying to do everything yourself without team
**Solution**: Board first, deals second. Never go alone.


# Company Structure & Legal Setup

Comprehensive guidance on structuring acquisition vehicles for maximum protection and flexibility.

## The Four Levels of Company Structuring

### Level 1: Partnership (Slab)
**Structure**: Partners share equally with no corporate protection
**Use Case**: Joint ventures, very small businesses up to $1M
**Limitations**: 
- No protection between partners and business liabilities
- Inflexible share and voting rights
- Not recommended for acquisitions

**Recommendation**: Always set up a company instead, even for small deals.

### Level 2: High Tower (Single Entity)
**Structure**: Proprietary Limited / LLC / Ltd / GmbH
- Shareholders own company directly
- Directors appointed as needed
- All operations through one entity

**Use Case**: Single acquisition or small portfolio (up to $30M total)

**Advantages**:
- Simple structure
- Clear ownership
- Liability protection via separate legal entity

**Limitations**:
- Becomes complex when diversifying
- All operations in one basket
- Difficult to manage different risk profiles
- Share dilution affects everything

**Setup**:
```
[Shareholders]
       ↓
  [Company Pty Ltd]
       ↓
  [Operations]
```

### Level 3: Three-Legged Stool (Recommended for Growth)
**Structure**: Holding company with specialized subsidiaries

**Components**:
1. **Top Entity**: You, family trust, or personal company
2. **Holding Company**: Owns all operating entities
3. **Trading Companies**: Earn operational income
4. **IP Companies**: Hold intellectual property and brand assets
5. **Investment Companies**: Hold investments (stocks, crypto, real estate)

**Three Layers of Protection**:
1. Trading companies protect operations from each other
2. Holding company protects assets from trading company risks
3. Top entity protects personal wealth from holding company

**Key Benefits**:
- Clear separation of operating risk and asset protection
- Directors/managers hired at operating entity level (not holding)
- Easy to issue shares in specific entities without diluting others
- Simplified risk management

**Setup**:
```
    [Personal Entity / Trust]
              ↓
       [Holding Company]
         ↓     ↓     ↓
    [Trading] [IP] [Investment]
     Companies Company Company
```

**Share Allocation Best Practice**:
- Issue maximum shares at incorporation (50,000 or 1M shares)
- Much easier to allocate 1,000 of 1M shares than reissue if you started with 100 shares
- Avoids costly restructures later
- Facilitates transitions to more complex structures

### Level 4: Octopus (Billion-Dollar Structure)
**Structure**: Holding company with multiple independent "arms"

**Architecture**:
```
       [Personal Entity]
              ↓
       [Holding Company]
         ↓   ↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
       [Arm 1] [Arm 2] [Arm 3] [Arm 4] [Arm N]
         ↓       ↓       ↓
    [Sub 1.1] [Sub 2.1] [Sub 3.1]
    [Sub 1.2] [Sub 2.2] [Sub 3.2]
```

**Key Principle**: Each arm is independent
- If one arm fails, others continue
- Like octopus losing one tentacle—organism survives
- Each arm can have its own board, management, capital structure

**Use Cases**:
- Multi-vertical roll-ups
- Geographic expansion (each region = arm)
- Different business models within industry
- Venture/investment arms separate from operating businesses

**Examples**:
- Arm 1: Healthcare clinics in Region A
- Arm 2: Healthcare clinics in Region B  
- Arm 3: Allied health services
- Arm 4: Medical real estate
- Arm 5: Healthcare technology investments

## Recommended Structure for Acquisitions

### Initial Setup (Pre-First Deal)

**Step 1: Create Personal Holding Company**
```
[You Personally]
       ↓
[YourName HoldCo Pty Ltd]
```

**Purpose**:
- This will be your permanent ownership vehicle
- Issue shares to board members at this level
- All acquisitions will be subsidiaries

**Share Structure Example**:
- Total Authorized Shares: 1,000,000
- Founder: 700,000 shares (70%)
- Board Chair: 100,000 shares (10%)
- Finance Lead: 75,000 shares (7.5%)
- Legal Lead: 75,000 shares (7.5%)
- Sector Expert: 50,000 shares (5%)

### Structure Per Acquisition

**For Each Deal**:
Create new Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) as subsidiary:

```
[YourName HoldCo Pty Ltd]
       ↓
[Target Business Acquisition SPV Pty Ltd]
```

**Why SPV Per Deal**:
- Isolates liability to that specific business
- Clean for due diligence if you sell individual assets
- Separate banking facilities per entity
- Clearer accounting and performance tracking
- Can bring in deal-specific investors without affecting other holdings

### Multi-Deal Structure Example

After closing 5 deals:

```
         [YourName HoldCo Pty Ltd]
    Board owns shares at this level
                  ↓
    ┌─────────────┼─────────────┬─────────────┐
    ↓             ↓             ↓             ↓
[Business 1   [Business 2   [Business 3   [Business 4
Acquisition   Acquisition   Acquisition   Acquisition
SPV]          SPV]          SPV]          SPV]
```

**Board Seat Location**: Board members sit on HoldCo board, not individual SPV boards

**Management**: Hire managers/directors at SPV level for day-to-day operations

## Incorporation Checklist

### Pre-Incorporation Decisions

**Jurisdiction Selection**:
- Incorporate where you'll operate (generally)
- Consider tax optimization (Delaware in US, specific states in Australia)
- Evaluate legal protection differences
- Factor in ongoing compliance costs

**Share Classes**:
- Ordinary shares (voting rights, dividends)
- Preference shares (priority on distributions, often non-voting)
- Consider if you'll need different classes for investors later

**Officers**:
- Directors (minimum 1-3 depending on jurisdiction)
- Secretary (often required)
- Shareholders (can be same as directors initially)

### Incorporation Documents Needed

**1. Constitution / Articles of Incorporation**
- Company purpose and powers
- Share classes and rights
- Director appointment/removal procedures
- Meeting requirements
- Dividend policies

**2. Shareholders Agreement**
- Transfer restrictions
- Pre-emptive rights
- Drag-along / tag-along provisions
- Deadlock resolution
- Exit mechanisms

**3. Initial Board Resolutions**
- Appoint directors and officers
- Adopt constitution
- Authorize share issuance
- Set up bank accounts
- Appoint auditors/accountants
- Approve registered office address

**4. Share Certificates**
- Document share ownership
- Keep in minute book
- Issue to all initial shareholders

### Ongoing Compliance

**Annual Requirements**:
- Annual General Meeting (AGM)
- Annual return/statement filing
- Financial statements preparation
- Tax returns
- Register updates (directors, shareholders, addresses)

**Record Keeping**:
- Minute book with all resolutions
- Register of directors
- Register of shareholders  
- Register of charges (if any secured lending)
- Copies of all material contracts

## Banking Setup

### Account Structure

**Holding Company**:
- Main operating account
- Savings/reserve account
- Consider credit card for expenses

**Each Acquisition SPV**:
- Trading account (all revenue flows here)
- Separate AP and AR if significant volume
- Loan payment account (if debt service)
- Consider separate payroll account

**Tax/Compliance Accounts**:
- Tax withholding account
- VAT/GST collection account
- Set aside percentage of revenue automatically

### Banking Relationships

**Before First Deal**:
1. Open accounts at 2-3 major banks
2. Establish business credit
3. Build relationship with business banker
4. Set up lines of credit (even small $10-20K initially)

**After First Deal**:
1. Transfer all target business banking to your entity
2. Notify all customers of new bank details
3. Update all payment processors
4. Redirect any direct debits
5. Close old entity accounts after 60-90 days

## Insurance Requirements

### Must-Have Policies

**1. Directors & Officers (D&O) Insurance**
- Protects board members from liability
- Essential for recruiting quality board
- Typical coverage: $1M-$5M
- Cost: ~$2-5K annually for small companies

**2. General Liability**
- Protects against third-party claims
- Required by most lease agreements
- Typical coverage: $2M-$5M
- Cost varies by industry

**3. Professional Indemnity** (if applicable)
- Required for professional services
- Healthcare, accounting, legal, consulting
- Coverage based on revenue
- Essential for healthcare acquisitions

**4. Workers Compensation**
- Mandatory in most jurisdictions if you have employees
- Covers workplace injuries
- Cost based on payroll and risk classification

**5. Cyber Insurance** (increasingly important)
- Covers data breaches
- Business interruption from cyber events
- Typical coverage: $1M-$5M
- Cost: $1-3K annually for small businesses

### Insurance Setup Timing

**Pre-Close**:
- D&O insurance in place
- General liability quoted and ready
- Professional indemnity quoted (if needed)

**At Settlement**:
- All policies activate same day as settlement
- Seller's policies terminate
- No coverage gap

**Post-Close**:
- Review adequacy of coverage
- Adjust based on actual operations
- Shop policies annually for better rates

## Tax Structure Optimization

### Holding Company Benefits

**Dividend Imputation**:
- Profits can flow up to HoldCo
- Tax paid at entity level may provide credits to shareholders
- Consult jurisdiction-specific rules

**Capital Gains Treatment**:
- Gains on sale of subsidiaries often have preferential treatment
- Small business CGT concessions may apply
- Holding period requirements in some jurisdictions

### Inter-Company Arrangements

**Management Fees**:
- HoldCo charges management fees to subsidiaries
- Moves profits to HoldCo level
- Covers actual services provided (board, finance, HR, IT)
- Must be arms-length pricing

**IP Licensing**:
- IP owned by IP company
- License to trading companies
- Royalty fees move profits to IP entity
- Can be tax-optimized depending on jurisdiction

**Debt Push-Down**:
- Acquisition debt can sit at HoldCo or SPV level
- Interest deductibility considerations
- Impacts DSCR calculations
- Consult with tax advisor on optimal structure

## Transaction Structure Considerations

### Asset vs Share Purchase

**Asset Purchase**:
**Buyer Advantages**:
- Cherry-pick assets you want
- Leave liabilities behind
- Step-up in asset basis (depreciation benefits)
- No hidden liabilities

**Buyer Disadvantages**:
- Must reestablish contracts
- May trigger transfer taxes
- Customers must be recontracted
- Lose historical tax attributes

**Share Purchase**:
**Buyer Advantages**:
- Everything transfers automatically
- Contracts continue uninterrupted
- Simpler transition
- Preserve tax losses (if valuable)

**Buyer Disadvantages**:
- Inherit all liabilities (known and unknown)
- No step-up in basis
- Must do more extensive due diligence

**Recommendation for No-Money-Down Deals**:
- Prefer asset purchase for control and risk management
- Accept share purchase if seller strongly prefers and due diligence is clean
- Get comprehensive reps and warranties either way
- Consider W&I insurance for larger deals

### Vendor Finance Documentation

**Key Deal Points**:
- Principal amount
- Interest rate (3-5% typical)
- Term (5-7 years)
- Payment frequency (monthly/quarterly)
- Payment holiday (6-12 months ideal)
- Security (aim for unsecured)
- Personal guarantee (aim to avoid)
- Prepayment rights (essential)
- Seller's remedies on default

**Critical Clauses**:
1. **Subordination**: Seller note subordinate to bank debt
2. **Non-recourse**: Liability limited to assets of SPV (no personal exposure)
3. **Prepayment**: Right to prepay without penalty
4. **Cross-default**: Ensure default doesn't trigger other agreements
5. **Reporting**: Quarterly financial reporting to seller

**Red Lines** (Don't Accept):
- Personal guarantee beyond SPV
- Seller's right to operate business on default (unless pure vendor finance with reclaim right)
- Blanket security over all current and future assets
- Covenants you can't control (e.g., customer concentration)

### Earnout Structures (When Appropriate)

**Use Case**: Bridge valuation gap or incentivize seller to help transition

**Typical Structure**:
- Base purchase price: 70-80% upfront (via vendor note or cash)
- Earnout: 20-30% tied to performance metrics
- Period: 1-3 years post-close
- Metrics: Revenue, EBITDA, customer retention (must be objective)

**Earnout Best Practices**:
- Keep it simple (one or two metrics maximum)
- Use revenue if possible (harder to manipulate than EBITDA)
- Cap at reasonable level (seller shouldn't have open-ended upside)
- Ensure you control operations (seller can't sabotage)
- Get monthly performance reporting

**Example**:
```
Purchase Price: $1,000,000
  - Base: $700,000 (vendor note, 7 years, 4% interest)
  - Earnout Year 1: Up to $100,000 if revenue >$X
  - Earnout Year 2: Up to $100,000 if revenue >$Y  
  - Earnout Year 3: Up to $100,000 if revenue >$Z
```
